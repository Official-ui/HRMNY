The situation

The music market is splitting in two.

AI is taking over cheap background music — fast, free, forgettable. Real human music, made by a named artist a brand can stand behind, is becoming the part worth paying for. The tell: AI has flooded supply, but not yet demand.

The gap between those two bars is the whole thesis: machines can make the music, but people still want the human one. Provably-human, fully-cleared supply gets more valuable as the commodity layer floods, not less.

Two things just changed. In July 2025, TikTok stopped letting brands use trending sounds in ads — they now need cleared music, and a TikTok license does not cover Instagram or YouTube; brands have already been sued over this.5,6 At the same time, the big labels started licensing AI music.4 So "human-made" now needs proof, not just a claim.

The plan

Four phases, in order.

Make payments work

Set up Stripe Connect so artists get paid. Add secure file delivery. Check every upload is real and human. Design the record now so it's ready to anchor later. Nothing ships until this works end to end.

Prove the payout promise

Onboard a first cohort through managers. Prove artists actually get paid, reliably, and that clearance integrity holds under real licenses. Trust is earned with completed transactions, not promises.

Press the opening

Lean into the two open windows — TikTok's cleared-music shift and the human-vs-AI split. Scale payout-ready supply and brand demand while the timing advantage lasts.

Anchor the record

Flip the switch: anchor the now-stable license and provenance record on Base, store contracts on Arweave. Proof turns on once there's something worth proving — a rebuild avoided, not deferred.

One key choice

Why the chain comes last.

A normal app keeps records in a database HRMNY controls. But the whole point of HRMNY is that you can trust the record without trusting HRMNY. The convergence

Where AI and Web3 actually meet.

Five independent threads are converging on one thesis — which happens to be HRMNY's: as AI floods the commodity end and AI buyers start needing rights-safe supply, provably-human, cleared, machine-licensable music gets more valuable, not less.

Provenance → table stakes

C2PA / Content Credentials is the winning standard — 6,000+ members (Google, Meta, OpenAI, Sony), now covering audio, and already required by sync buyers. By Aug 2, 2026 the EU AI Act mandates machine-readable markings on AI audio.9 So adopt the standard; let the moat be the combination — provenance + fingerprint + attestation + the accumulating record.

Copyright fog holds

UMG settled with Udio, Warner with Suno — but UMG and Sony are still litigating, with a pivotal fair-use ruling expected summer 2026, and indie artists filed separate class actions.4 The fog won't lift before launch, and prompt-only AI still gets no copyright.3 "Human-made is the safer choice" is now an industry talking point.

On-chain IP, no token

Story Protocol made programmable IP a funded category — borrow the architecture (anchor to Base), reject the tokenomics. Keep the binding license off-chain and legal; the chain is only a tamper-proof anchor. Never make the smart contract the license.

Agents become buyers

Machine-readable licensing (RSL, an open standard since Dec 2025) and agentic-commerce rails (x402, ~$50M cumulative by Apr 2026) point to the real wedge: a brand's content agent that can discover, license, pay for, and prove rights to a track autonomously — and the cleanest supply for that agent is provably-human, cleared, machine-licensable music.11 Design for it now; build it when a buyer asks.

avoid as hype: tokenized royalties, smart-contract-as-license, and building agentic licensing before there's a buyer asking for it.

The honest read

SWOT, after the research.

Where HRMNY is strong, where it's exposed, and what the evidence says. Filter the grid.

Strengths
  • Legally cleaner asset. Prompt-only AI music gets no copyright,3 so human music is the only ownable, licensable thing — HRMNY only sells that.
  • Solves a proven pain. One license clears every short-form platform, directly answering the cross-platform gap brands are being sued over.5,6
  • Most artist-favorable terms. 100% to the artist + a transparent 20% buyer fee, vs incumbents' 25–50% / ~70%-to-rightsholder cuts.7
  • Capital-efficient. Buy/adopt Stripe, AudD, C2PA, Base, Arweave; build only the record and the matching.9,12,13,14
Weaknesses
  • Small core market. Recorded-music sync is ~$641M and just turned negative (−2.0%).1
  • The gate is unbuilt. No Stripe Connect = can't transact; classic marketplace cold-start, no liquidity yet.
  • Catalog lacks hits. Like Epidemic, a humans-only catalog has no mainstream songs buyers recognize.7
  • Provenance alone isn't a moat. C2PA certifies history, not truth, and is becoming table stakes.9
Opportunities
  • The human/AI split is the axis. Artlist went fully generative; "verified human at scale, 100% to artist" is open ground.7,8
  • A forcing event. TikTok's July 2025 CML shift + the litigation wave make brands need pre-cleared, multi-platform music now.5,6
  • Scarcity premium rising. AI is 44% of uploads but ~2% of streams — provable-human is the scarce good.2
  • Agents as future buyers. Machine-readable, rights-safe supply is exactly what agentic commerce will pay for.11
Threats
  • Fast-follow incumbents. Epidemic is already "human-made" and launched an AI tool + bigger payouts; it could bolt on attestation.7
  • Label AI walled gardens. UMG/Udio and Warner/Suno legitimize licensed AI and could capture "premium AI."4
  • The wedge already exists. SyncRep's $99-lifetime, 0%-commission undercuts the pricing edge among small players.7
  • Legal ground is shifting. The summer-2026 fair-use ruling could reset the board overnight.4
